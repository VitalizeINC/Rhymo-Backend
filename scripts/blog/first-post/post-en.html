Rhymo in one sentence

Rhymo is a calm writing desk for Persian poetry, songs and rap: four tools in one app that hand work to each other, from rhyme to word bank, from word bank to notepad.

  • Rhyme finder: type one word and get its rhymes, by sound or by spelling.

  • Syllables: see the syllables and vowels of any word, and work on your meter.

  • Word bank: keep good words in folders you name yourself.

  • Notepad: write your lines and watch each rhyme group take its own color.

Rhyme is made of sound, not letters

Persian is written mostly without short vowels, so two words can look nothing alike and still rhyme perfectly when you say them out loud. Hezâr (هزار, "a thousand") and bahâr (بهار, "spring") share no visible ending on paper, yet the ear hears the same final sound. That is why the heart of Rhymo is a phonetic rhyme finder: it knows how a word is pronounced and builds rhymes from its final sound.

Classical poetry has its own rule, though. When you want rhymes that also share the same final letters, switch to literary mode, and hezâr sits next to bâzâr (بازار) and golzâr (گلزار) instead.

A few things make it precise:

  • No vowel marks needed. Type the word the way you always write it. If you do add vowel marks, Rhymo keeps them.

  • "Which pronunciation?" When a word can be read more than one way, Rhymo asks which one you mean.

  • You choose the rhyme length. Tap the syllables to make the rhyme shorter or longer. This is what makes multi-syllable rhymes possible.

  • Consonant filter and homophones. Keep only rhymes with the letters you want, or see words that sound the same side by side.

Any rhyme you like goes to your word bank with one tap, straight into the folder you choose.

Syllable by syllable, until the meter fits

Sometimes the rhyme is right but the line still limps: one syllable too many, or one too few. In Syllables, type a word and see how many parts it has and which vowel each part carries. Bahâr has two: ba and hâr. Khiyâboon (خیابون, "street") has three: khi, yâ and boon. With the syllables in front of you, counting beats and fitting words to a meter, or to a beat, gets much easier.

Word bank: a home for the words you can't lose

Every writer has words waiting for the right poem. In Rhymo you keep them in folders you name yourself: "love songs", "dark rhymes", or the name of the album in your head. Words arrive from the rhyme screen or straight from the notepad, and search brings them back when you need them.

Notepad: where rhymes get their colors

Rhymo's notepad is made for the middle of writing, and everything you type is saved by itself. Three simple keys do all the work:

  • "/" starts the next line.

  • "|word|" marks a rhyme and colors it.

  • "||" starts a new rhyme group with a new color, up to 12 in one note.

Colors that sit next to each other are kept far apart on purpose, so groups never blur together. You can read the rhyme scheme of a verse or a ghazal at a glance. And while a "|" is open, rhyme ideas appear at the bottom of the screen: from the whole dictionary, or only from your own folders.

From the ghazal to rap

A ghazal walks slowly and rap runs fast, but both speak the same language: sound. Take this famous couplet by Hafez:

یوسف گم‌گشته بازآید به کنعان غم مخور
کلبهٔ احزان شود روزی گلستان غم مخور

Lost Joseph will return to Canaan, do not grieve. This house of sorrow will one day be a rose garden, do not grieve.

Kan'ân and golestân are the rhyme, and gham makhor ("do not grieve") is the radif: words repeated exactly after the rhyme in every line. Rap carries the same logic at a faster tempo, with multi-syllable rhymes like divooneh and mimooneh, or khiyâboon and biyâboon. Rhymo is built for both: the same tools, on a different beat.

A dictionary that grows with you

Rhymo already knows more than 16,000 reviewed Persian words, and the list keeps growing. When it doesn't know a word, it asks whether to add it. Suggested words join the dictionary after review, so the rhyme finder gets better for everyone.

Calm, Persian, and yours

  • Persian from day one: a right-to-left interface, the Vazirmatn typeface, and a rhyme engine built for Persian sounds.

  • Easy on the eyes: a dark, quiet design for long writing sessions that respects your "Reduce Motion" setting.

  • Your writing stays yours: notes and word banks are saved only to your own account, and you can delete your account at any time.

  • No ads: there are no ads in the app, and we don't sell your data.

Who is Rhymo for?

  • Lyricists who want a fresh rhyme, not the obvious one.

  • Rappers who care about multi-syllable rhymes and flow on the beat.

  • Poets working with meter, rhyme and radif.

  • Teachers and students of Persian literature who want to see syllables and rhyme, not just memorize them.

  • Anyone with a notebook full of half-finished lines, waiting for the last word.

How do I start?

  1. Get Rhymo free on the App Store, for iPhone and iPad on iOS 16.4 or later. Android is coming soon.

  2. Sign in with Apple, Google or email. For email, we send you a 6-digit code.

  3. Tap "یافتن قافیه" (Find rhymes) and type your first word.

  4. Send the good rhymes to your word bank, then write in the notepad.
