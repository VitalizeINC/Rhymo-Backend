You need something that lands on the same sound, carries the meaning, and doesn't sound like the first rhyme everyone reaches for. Rhymo in English is being built for exactly that moment: a rhyme finder and lyric notebook that thinks in sound, not spelling.

What Rhymo does

Rhymo is a calm writing desk for songs, rap and poetry: four tools that hand work to each other, from rhyme to word bank, from word bank to notepad.

  • Rhyme finder: perfect rhymes, near rhymes and multi-syllable rhymes for any word.

  • Syllables: see every syllable of a word and where the stress falls.

  • Word bank: keep good words in folders you name yourself.

  • Notepad: write your bars and watch each rhyme group take its own color.

A rhyme finder that listens

English spelling lies about sound. Love and move look like a rhyme and aren't. Rhythm and algorithm look like strangers and rhyme perfectly. So Rhymo doesn't compare letters. It looks up how each word is pronounced, finds the stressed vowel, and matches everything from there to the end of the word. Under the hood is the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary: about 126,000 English words, each with its sounds and its stress.

That gives you three ways to rhyme any word. Take money:

  • Perfect rhymes share every sound from the stressed vowel on: honey, funny, sunny, bunny.

  • Near rhymes keep the vowels and loosen the consonants: country, buddy, dusty, fuzzy. They are the backbone of modern rap and pop, where the ear follows vowels more than spelling.

  • Multi-syllable rhymes match the last two syllables or more: company, harmony, destiny, agony.

And when you do want letters, for eye rhymes or a visual trick on the page, switch to spelling mode and Rhymo matches the final letters instead.

Watch it work

Type midnight. Rhymo splits it into mid · night, sees that the rhyme lives in -ight, and hands you the perfect rhymes first: daylight, delight, bright, flight, candlelight, flashlight. Then come the near rhymes, like sliced and spiced, for when you want the sound without the cliché.

A few controls make it sharper:

  • Tap the syllables to make the rhyme deeper or shallower, from a one-syllable echo to a full multi-syllable match.

  • Filter by letters to keep only rhymes with the consonants you want in the line.

  • Rhyme whole phrases, not just single words, when your line ends on two words.

  • Save with one tap. Any rhyme you like goes straight to your word bank, into the folder you choose.

Syllables and stress

A rhyme can be right and the line can still feel wrong, because the stress lands in the wrong place. Rhymo shows the syllables of every word and which one carries the stress: MON·ey, for·EV·er, MID·night. The rhyme begins at the last stressed syllable (in midnight that's night, with its lighter stress), which is why forever rhymes with never, clever and whatever, and sits close to better and center. Once you can see the stress, fitting words to a beat or a meter stops being guesswork.

Word bank: a home for the words you can't lose

Good words show up at bad times: on the train, in the shower, halfway through someone else's song. Keep them in folders you name yourself: "Hooks", "Late night", or the title of the record in your head. Words arrive from the rhyme finder or straight from the notepad, and search brings them back the moment you need them.

Notepad: rhymes in color

Rhymo's notepad is made for the middle of writing, and it saves everything by itself. Three keys do all the work:

  • "/" starts the next bar.

  • "|word|" marks a rhyme and colors it.

  • "||" starts a new rhyme group with a new color, up to 12 in one note.

Colors that sit side by side are kept far apart, so groups never blur together, and your whole rhyme scheme reads at a glance. While a "|" is open, rhyme ideas appear at the bottom of the screen, from the whole dictionary or only from your own folders.

Built for songs and for rap

A ballad and a sixteen-bar verse ask different things of a rhyme. The ballad wants the clean, perfect match. The verse wants density: near rhymes stacked inside the line, multi-syllable chains that keep the flow rolling across the beat. Rhymo gives you both from the same search, so you choose the sound instead of settling for the first word that fits.
